Belgian, Liège & American Waffles

Not all waffles are the same. Here are the three big families, what makes each one special, and how to make them at home.

1American waffles

Thin, round or square, and leavened with baking powder. The batter is quick to mix and the waffles cook fast in a shallow iron. They are soft and tender, perfect for stacking with butter and syrup on a busy morning.

2Belgian (Brussels) waffles

Light, tall and crisp, with deep pockets.

  • Often made with yeast or whipped egg whites for lift.
  • Cooked in a deep-grid iron.
  • The crisp shell and airy middle hold toppings well.

3Liège waffles

Made from a rich, brioche-like yeasted dough rather than a pourable batter. Pearl sugar is folded in just before cooking and caramelises on the hot plates, giving a crunchy, chewy waffle that needs no syrup at all.

4Choosing your iron

A shallow iron suits American batter; a deep iron suits Belgian. Liège dough works in a Belgian iron but leaves sugar on the plates, so clean them while still warm with a damp cloth.

5Make-ahead tips

Cool waffles on a rack, not a plate, so steam can escape. Freeze them in a single layer, then reheat in a toaster or hot oven. They come back crisp, which makes weekday waffles easy.

quick tip

Liège dough is easiest to handle cold: shape the balls, then rest them for ten minutes before cooking.
